# Numerical discrepancies

- Al-A'raf (7:54) + Fussilat (41:9-12) - Creation takes six days versus eight if durations are added; classical exegesis counts the four days inclusively. | Explanation: The first passage gives six days for creation. Adding the two, four and two days in the second gives eight; classical commentary reads the four days as including the first two, yielding six instead. | https://quran.com/en/fussilat/9/tafsirs
# Contextual tensions

- Al-Baqarah (2:62) + Ali 'Imran (3:85) - Promises reward to righteous believers of several communities, but rejects other religion; interpretations of Islam and chronology differ. | Explanation: The first passage promises reward to righteous believers from several religious communities. The second rejects religion other than Islam. Whether these statements conflict depends on the meaning of Islam and the scope and chronology of the promises. | https://quran.com/3/85 | https://previous.quran.com/2%3A62/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran

# Contextual tensions

- An-Nisa (4:78) + An-Nisa (4:79) - Successive verses attribute misfortunes to God and to the individual. | Explanation: The first says that all things are from God; the second attributes good to God and evil to oneself. Commentators can distinguish divine causation from human responsibility.
- Al-An'am (6:164) + An-Nahl (16:25) - No soul bears another's burden, yet misleaders bear burdens of those they mislead. | Explanation: The first verse states individual responsibility; the second assigns additional burdens to those who lead others astray. These can be reconciled if the additional burden is punishment for the misleader's own conduct.
- Al-Baqarah (2:256) + At-Tawbah (9:4-6) - The prohibition of religious compulsion sits alongside a command to fight specified polytheists. | Explanation: The first prohibits compulsion in religion; the latter addresses hostilities after sacred months, exempts treaty-keepers and requires protection for those seeking refuge. The scope of warfare and conversion determines whether a tension remains.
- Al-Baqarah (2:29) + An-Nazi'at (79:27-33) - The sequence of heaven and Earth's preparation appears to differ between creation accounts. | Explanation: The first passage describes earthly creation before formation of the heavens; the second describes heaven before spreading Earth and producing its resources. Classical exegesis distinguishes initial creation from later spreading. | https://previous.quran.com/2%3A29/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir?locale=en
- Al-Anfal (8:65) + Al-Anfal (8:66) - The expected ratio of victorious believers to opponents changes from one-to-ten to one-to-two. | Explanation: The first promises victory to 20 steadfast believers over 200; the next gives 100 over 200 and explicitly says God has lightened the burden. The text presents a change in obligation, not an unexplained arithmetic contradiction.
- Al-Baqarah (2:106) + Al-An'am (6:115) - The replacement of revelations raises a tension with the assertion that God's words cannot be changed. | Explanation: The first permits abrogating or causing a sign to be forgotten and replacing it; the second says none can change God's words. Divine replacement and unauthorized alteration can be distinguished.
- As-Sajdah (32:5) + Al-Ma'arij (70:4) - Divine or angelic ascent is associated with days of one thousand and fifty thousand years. | Explanation: The first describes an affair ascending in a day measured as 1,000 years; the second describes angels and the Spirit ascending in a day measured as 50,000 years. They may refer to distinct events or symbolic durations.
