# Numerical discrepancies

- Al-A'raf (7:54) + Fussilat (41:9-12) - Creation is described in six days and in periods of two, four and two days. | Explanation: Al-A'raf gives six days. Fussilat gives two days for Earth, four for its provisions and two for the heavens. The numbers add to eight if the four days are additional; the passage does not explicitly say they are additional. | https://quran.com/en/fussilat/9/tafsirs

# Contextual tensions

- Al-Baqarah (2:62) + Ali 'Imran (3:85) - Several religious communities are promised reward, while religion other than Islam is rejected. | Explanation: Al-Baqarah promises reward to believers, Jews, Christians and Sabians who believe in God and the Last Day and act righteously. Ali 'Imran rejects religion other than Islam. | https://quran.com/3/85 | https://previous.quran.com/2%3A62/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran
- An-Nisa (4:78) + An-Nisa (4:79) - Successive verses attribute misfortunes to God and to the individual. | Explanation: Verse 78 says all things come from God; verse 79 attributes good to God and evil to oneself.
- Al-An'am (6:164) + An-Nahl (16:25) - No soul bears another's burden, yet misleaders bear some burdens of those they mislead. | Explanation: Al-An'am states that no burdened soul bears another's burden. An-Nahl says misleaders bear their own burdens in full and some burdens of those they mislead.
- Al-Baqarah (2:256) + At-Tawbah (9:4-6) - The prohibition of religious compulsion stands alongside commands to fight specified polytheists. | Explanation: Al-Baqarah prohibits religious compulsion. At-Tawbah orders fighting polytheists after the sacred months, exempts treaty-keepers and requires protection for those seeking refuge.
- Al-Baqarah (2:29) + An-Nazi'at (79:27-33) - Earth's creation and preparation are placed differently relative to the heavens. | Explanation: Al-Baqarah describes creating what is on Earth before forming the seven heavens. An-Nazi'at describes building heaven before spreading Earth and bringing forth its water and pasture. | https://previous.quran.com/2%3A29/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir?locale=en
- Al-Anfal (8:65) + Al-Anfal (8:66) - The stated ratio of victorious believers to opponents changes from one-to-ten to one-to-two. | Explanation: Verse 65 promises victory to twenty steadfast believers over two hundred; verse 66 gives one hundred over two hundred and explicitly says God has lightened the burden.
- Al-Baqarah (2:106) + Al-An'am (6:115) - Revelations can be replaced, while God's words are described as unchangeable. | Explanation: Al-Baqarah describes God replacing a revelation that is abrogated or forgotten. Al-An'am says none can change God's words.
- As-Sajdah (32:5) + Al-Ma'arij (70:4) - Divine and angelic ascent is associated with days of one thousand and fifty thousand years. | Explanation: As-Sajdah describes affairs ascending in a day measured as 1,000 years. Al-Ma'arij describes angels and the Spirit ascending in a day measured as 50,000 years.
- Al-Baqarah (2:48) + Ta-Ha (20:109) - Intercession is denied in one passage and permitted with divine authorization in another. | Explanation: Al-Baqarah warns of a day when intercession will not be accepted. Ta-Ha states that intercession will benefit only those authorized by the Merciful.
- An-Nisa (4:48) + Az-Zumar (39:53-54) - Associating partners with God is described as unforgivable, while all sins are promised forgiveness. | Explanation: An-Nisa excludes association with God from forgiveness while allowing other sins to be forgiven at God's will. Az-Zumar promises forgiveness of all sins and calls people to return to God.
- Al-An'am (6:14) + Al-A'raf (7:143) - Muhammad and Moses are each described using claims of priority in submission or belief. | Explanation: Muhammad is commanded to be the first to submit; Moses declares himself the first of the believers after the mountain episode. The expressions concern different speakers and circumstances.
- Al-Insan (76:29-30) + Al-Kahf (18:29) - Human choice in belief is affirmed alongside dependence on God's will. | Explanation: Al-Kahf invites whoever wills to believe or disbelieve. Al-Insan says people cannot will unless God wills.
- An-Nahl (16:93) + Al-Muddaththir (74:38) - God determines guidance and misguidance, while each soul is held accountable for its deeds. | Explanation: An-Nahl states that God leads astray and guides whom he wills, and that people will be questioned about their deeds. Al-Muddaththir holds each soul in pledge for what it earned.
- Al-An'am (6:12) + As-Sajdah (32:13) - Divine mercy is affirmed alongside a declared intention to fill Hell. | Explanation: Al-An'am says God has prescribed mercy for himself. As-Sajdah says God could have guided every soul but declares Hell will be filled with jinn and humans.
- Al-Baqarah (2:219) + An-Nisa (4:43) + Al-Ma'idah (5:90-91) - Alcohol is treated through three different prescriptions. | Explanation: Al-Baqarah acknowledges benefits but says the sin is greater. An-Nisa prohibits approaching prayer while intoxicated. Al-Ma'idah orders avoidance of intoxicants.
- Al-Baqarah (2:115) + Al-Baqarah (2:144) - God's presence is associated with every direction, while prayer is directed toward a particular sanctuary. | Explanation: Verse 115 says God's face is wherever one turns. Verse 144 commands turning toward the Sacred Mosque.
- Al-Qiyamah (75:22-23) + Al-An'am (6:103) - Faces look toward God in the afterlife, while vision is described as unable to encompass him. | Explanation: Al-Qiyamah describes radiant faces looking toward their Lord. Al-An'am states that sight cannot perceive or encompass God, while he perceives all sight.
