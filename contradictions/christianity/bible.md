# Numerical discrepancies

- 2 Samuel 24:9 + 1 Chronicles 21:5 - David’s census gives different troop totals for Israel and Judah. The counting scope may differ. | Explanation: Samuel gives 800,000 for Israel and 500,000 for Judah; Chronicles gives 1,100,000 and 470,000 respectively. The totals differ, although the two accounts may count different groups.
- 2 Samuel 10:18 + 1 Chronicles 19:18 - The parallel accounts give 700 versus 7,000 chariot-related casualties; manuscript and translation differences matter. | Explanation: The parallel battle accounts report 700 versus 7,000 chariot-related losses. The distinction between chariots and their crews, and possible copying or translation differences, should be considered.
# Conflicting accounts

- Matthew 27:3-10 + Acts 1:18-19 - The accounts differ on Judas’s death and field purchase; a combined sequence is a possible harmonization. | Explanation: Matthew describes Judas hanging himself and the priests buying the field. Acts attributes the field to Judas and describes his fatal fall. A sequence combining both accounts is possible but is not stated in either passage.

# Numerical discrepancies

- 2 Kings 8:26 + 2 Chronicles 22:2 - Ahaziah's age at accession differs between manuscript traditions. | Explanation: Kings gives 22 years; the received Hebrew text of Chronicles gives 42. Some translations follow ancient versions reading 22, so the discrepancy is edition-dependent. | https://openwordbible.org/variants/
- 2 Kings 24:8 + 2 Chronicles 36:9 - Jehoiachin's age at accession differs between manuscripts and translations. | Explanation: Kings gives 18 years; the received Hebrew text of Chronicles gives eight. Some witnesses and translations give 18 in Chronicles as well, suggesting a transmission problem. | https://openwordbible.org/variants/
- Ezra 2:6 + Nehemiah 7:11 - Parallel lists give different numbers of descendants of Pahath-Moab. | Explanation: Ezra gives 2,812 people, while Nehemiah gives 2,818. The lists may reflect different records or a copying discrepancy.
- Genesis 46:27 + Acts 7:14 - Jacob's family totals 70 or 75 people in the accounts of migration to Egypt. | Explanation: Genesis in the Hebrew tradition gives 70, while Stephen's speech in Acts gives 75. The Greek Septuagint also gives 75, explaining the difference between textual traditions. | https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+46%3A27%2CActs+7%3A14&version=NET

# Conflicting accounts

- 2 Samuel 24:1 + 1 Chronicles 21:1 - David's census is prompted by the Lord in Samuel and by Satan in Chronicles. | Explanation: The parallel narratives attribute the incitement to different agents. A theological harmonization can distinguish ultimate divine permission from an intermediary's action, but the passages themselves emphasize different agents.
- 1 Samuel 17:49-51 + 2 Samuel 21:19 + 1 Chronicles 20:5 - David and Elhanan are credited with killing Goliath in different textual accounts. | Explanation: David kills Goliath in 1 Samuel; the received Hebrew text of 2 Samuel names Elhanan. Chronicles names Goliath's brother instead. Some translations of 2 Samuel adopt the latter reading. | https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Samuel+21%3A19&version=NET
- Matthew 1:6-16 + Luke 3:23-31 - Jesus' genealogies give Joseph different fathers and trace Davidic descent through different sons. | Explanation: Matthew names Jacob as Joseph's father and traces the line through Solomon. Luke names Heli and traces it through Nathan. Legal versus biological ancestry or Mary's lineage are proposed reconciliations, not explicit in the passages. | https://bible.org/book/export/html/6313
- Matthew 28:1-8 + Mark 16:1-8 + Luke 24:1-10 - The empty-tomb narratives differ in visitors, messengers and the women's reported reactions. | Explanation: Matthew describes an angel and two named women; Mark describes a young man and three women; Luke describes two men and several women. Selective reporting could explain some differences, but the narrative details are not identical.
- Matthew 2:13-23 + Luke 2:22-39 - Jesus' infancy narratives describe flight to Egypt versus a return to Nazareth after temple rites. | Explanation: Matthew narrates a flight to Egypt before settling in Nazareth. Luke reports temple rites followed by a return to Nazareth. A combined chronology requires events omitted by one or both accounts.

# Contextual tensions

- Exodus 33:11 + Exodus 33:20-23 - Moses speaks with God face to face, yet cannot see God's face and live. | Explanation: The earlier verse describes direct conversation; the later verses deny the possibility of seeing God's face and surviving. The expression face to face may describe intimacy rather than literal sight.
- James 2:24 + Romans 3:28 - James emphasizes works in justification, while Paul emphasizes faith apart from works of the law. | Explanation: James rejects justification by faith alone, while Paul contrasts faith with works of the law. Different meanings of works, faith and justification are central to proposed theological reconciliations.
- Proverbs 26:4 + Proverbs 26:5 - Adjacent proverbs advise both answering and not answering a fool according to his folly. | Explanation: The two sayings prescribe opposite responses for different stated reasons. Read as situational wisdom, they can complement each other rather than constitute universal contradictory rules.
