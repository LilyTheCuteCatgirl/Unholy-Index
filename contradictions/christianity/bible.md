# Numerical discrepancies

- 2 Samuel 24:9 + 1 Chronicles 21:5 - David's census gives different troop totals for Israel and Judah. | Explanation: Samuel counts 800,000 for Israel and 500,000 for Judah; Chronicles counts 1,100,000 and 470,000 respectively.
- 2 Samuel 10:18 + 1 Chronicles 19:18 - The parallel battle accounts report 700 versus 7,000 chariot-related losses. | Explanation: Samuel reports 700 chariots and 40,000 horsemen; Chronicles reports 7,000 chariot-related losses and 40,000 foot soldiers in common editions.
- 2 Kings 8:26 + 2 Chronicles 22:2 - Ahaziah's age at accession differs between textual traditions. | Explanation: Kings gives 22 years; the received Hebrew text of Chronicles gives 42. Some editions of Chronicles read 22 instead. | https://openwordbible.org/variants/
- 2 Kings 24:8 + 2 Chronicles 36:9 - Jehoiachin's age at accession differs between textual traditions. | Explanation: Kings gives 18 years; the received Hebrew text of Chronicles gives eight. Some editions of Chronicles give 18. | https://openwordbible.org/variants/
- Ezra 2:6 + Nehemiah 7:11 - Parallel lists give different numbers of descendants of Pahath-Moab. | Explanation: Ezra counts 2,812 people; Nehemiah counts 2,818.
- Genesis 46:27 + Acts 7:14 - Jacob's family totals 70 or 75 people in accounts of migration to Egypt. | Explanation: Genesis in the Hebrew tradition counts 70; Acts counts 75. The Greek Septuagint version of Genesis also counts 75. | https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+46%3A27%2CActs+7%3A14&version=NET
- 1 Kings 7:26 + 2 Chronicles 4:5 - The bronze basin of Solomon's temple has two reported capacities. | Explanation: Kings gives 2,000 baths; Chronicles gives 3,000 baths. Both describe the same temple basin.
- 1 Kings 4:26 + 2 Chronicles 9:25 - Solomon's horse stalls number 40,000 or 4,000 in the received Hebrew text. | Explanation: The Hebrew text of Kings counts 40,000 stalls; Chronicles counts 4,000. Some translations of Kings give 4,000. | https://new.bible.org/article/introduction-first-and-second-chronicles
- 2 Samuel 23:8 + 1 Chronicles 11:11 - Parallel descriptions credit David's chief warrior with different numbers of defeated opponents. | Explanation: Samuel gives 800 in a single encounter; Chronicles gives 300. Names and titles also differ across editions.
- Ezra 2:5 + Nehemiah 7:10 - The lists give different numbers of descendants of Arah. | Explanation: Ezra counts 775; Nehemiah counts 652.
- Ezra 2:8 + Nehemiah 7:13 - The lists give different numbers of descendants of Zattu. | Explanation: Ezra counts 945; Nehemiah counts 845.
- Ezra 2:12 + Nehemiah 7:17 - The lists give different numbers of descendants of Azgad. | Explanation: Ezra counts 1,222; Nehemiah counts 2,322.
- Ezra 2:15 + Nehemiah 7:20 - The lists give different numbers of descendants of Adin. | Explanation: Ezra counts 454; Nehemiah counts 655.
- Ezra 2:19 + Nehemiah 7:22 - The lists give different numbers of descendants of Hashum. | Explanation: Ezra counts 223; Nehemiah counts 328.
- Ezra 2:28 + Nehemiah 7:32 - The lists give different population totals for Bethel and Ai. | Explanation: Ezra counts 223 people; Nehemiah counts 123.
- Ezra 2:65 + Nehemiah 7:67 - The lists give different numbers of male and female singers. | Explanation: Ezra counts 200 singers; Nehemiah counts 245.
- 2 Kings 25:8 + Jeremiah 52:12 - The Babylonian commander's arrival in Jerusalem is dated three days apart. | Explanation: Kings dates the arrival to the seventh day of the fifth month; Jeremiah gives the tenth day of that month. | https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Kings+25%3A8%3B+Jeremiah+52%3A12&version=WEB
- 2 Kings 25:17 + Jeremiah 52:22 - The height of the bronze pillar capitals differs between accounts. | Explanation: Kings gives three cubits for the capital; Jeremiah gives five cubits.
- 2 Kings 25:19 + Jeremiah 52:25 - The accounts give different numbers of royal advisers captured in Jerusalem. | Explanation: Kings names five men who saw the king's face; Jeremiah names seven.
- 1 Kings 9:28 + 2 Chronicles 8:18 - Solomon's expedition to Ophir brings back different quantities of gold. | Explanation: Kings records 420 talents of gold; Chronicles records 450 talents.

# Conflicting accounts

- Matthew 27:3-10 + Acts 1:18-19 - The accounts differ on Judas's death and purchase of the field. | Explanation: Matthew describes Judas hanging himself and priests buying a field with the returned silver. Acts attributes the field to Judas and describes his fatal fall.
- 2 Samuel 24:1 + 1 Chronicles 21:1 - David's census is prompted by the Lord in Samuel and by Satan in Chronicles. | Explanation: The two accounts attribute the incitement of David to different agents.
- 1 Samuel 17:49-51 + 2 Samuel 21:19 + 1 Chronicles 20:5 - Different textual accounts credit David or Elhanan with killing Goliath. | Explanation: David kills Goliath in 1 Samuel; the received Hebrew text of 2 Samuel credits Elhanan. Chronicles identifies Elhanan's victim as Goliath's brother; some translations of Samuel follow that reading. | https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Samuel+21%3A19&version=NET
- Matthew 1:6-16 + Luke 3:23-31 - Jesus' genealogies give Joseph different fathers and trace descent through different sons of David. | Explanation: Matthew names Jacob as Joseph's father and traces the line through Solomon. Luke names Heli and traces the line through Nathan. | https://bible.org/book/export/html/6313
- Matthew 28:1-8 + Mark 16:1-8 + Luke 24:1-10 - The empty-tomb narratives differ in visitors, messengers and the women's reactions. | Explanation: Matthew describes two named women and an angel; Mark names three women and a young man; Luke describes several women and two men. Mark reports silence, whereas Luke reports the women telling the apostles.
- Matthew 2:13-23 + Luke 2:22-39 - Jesus' infancy narratives give different accounts of the family's journey to Nazareth. | Explanation: Matthew describes flight to Egypt and later settlement in Nazareth; Luke describes the temple rites in Jerusalem followed by a return to Nazareth.
- Matthew 8:28-34 + Mark 5:1-13 + Luke 8:26-33 - The accounts of demons entering swine describe different numbers of possessed men. | Explanation: Matthew describes two men emerging from tombs; Mark and Luke focus on one man. All three describe the demons entering pigs.
- Matthew 9:18-25 + Mark 5:22-24, 5:35-43 - Jairus's daughter is already dead in Matthew but initially dying in Mark. | Explanation: Matthew has the father report that his daughter has just died. Mark has Jairus report that she is near death, with news of her death arriving later.
- Matthew 20:29-34 + Mark 10:46-52 + Luke 18:35-43 - The accounts differ on the number of blind men and the direction of travel near Jericho. | Explanation: Matthew describes two blind men as Jesus leaves Jericho; Mark describes one named Bartimaeus as he leaves; Luke describes one as Jesus approaches the city.
- Matthew 21:1-7 + Mark 11:1-7 - The accounts of Jesus entering Jerusalem describe different animals being brought. | Explanation: Matthew reports a donkey and its colt; Mark describes a single colt being brought for Jesus to ride.
- Matthew 27:44 + Luke 23:39-43 - Matthew describes both crucified criminals insulting Jesus; Luke describes one defending him. | Explanation: Matthew says the robbers crucified with Jesus insult him. Luke records one criminal insulting Jesus and the other rebuking him and requesting remembrance.
- Matthew 27:32 + John 19:17 - The crucifixion narratives identify different bearers of Jesus' cross. | Explanation: Matthew describes Simon of Cyrene being compelled to carry the cross; John describes Jesus going out carrying his cross.
- Mark 15:25 + John 19:14-16 - The timing of the crucifixion differs between Mark and John. | Explanation: Mark places the crucifixion at the third hour. John places Jesus before Pilate at about the sixth hour, before he is handed over for crucifixion.
- Matthew 26:34 + Mark 14:30 - Jesus' prediction of Peter's denial specifies different numbers of cockcrows. | Explanation: Matthew predicts three denials before the rooster crows; Mark specifies before the rooster crows twice.
- Matthew 27:46-50 + Luke 23:46 + John 19:30 - The Gospel accounts record different final utterances of Jesus on the cross. | Explanation: Matthew records a cry of abandonment followed by another loud cry; Luke records Jesus entrusting his spirit to the Father; John records Jesus declaring completion.
- Luke 24:50-53 + Acts 1:3-12 - Luke and Acts narrate Jesus' ascension with different chronological detail. | Explanation: Luke places the ascension in the closing resurrection narrative without specifying a forty-day interval. Acts explicitly describes appearances over forty days before the ascension.

# Contextual tensions

- Exodus 33:11 + Exodus 33:20-23 - Moses speaks with God face to face, yet cannot see God's face and live. | Explanation: The first passage describes direct conversation face to face. The second says that no human can see God's face and survive, permitting Moses to see only his back.
- James 2:24 + Romans 3:28 - James emphasizes works in justification; Paul emphasizes faith apart from works of the law. | Explanation: James says justification is not by faith alone; Paul says people are justified through faith apart from works of the law.
- Proverbs 26:4 + Proverbs 26:5 - Adjacent proverbs prescribe opposite responses to a fool's folly. | Explanation: One warns against answering a fool lest the reader become like him; the next urges answering him lest he consider himself wise.
- Numbers 23:19 + Genesis 6:6-7 - God is described as not repenting, yet regrets making humanity. | Explanation: Numbers distinguishes God from humans who change their minds; Genesis attributes regret over humanity's creation to God.
- 1 Samuel 15:11 + 1 Samuel 15:29, 15:35 - The same chapter attributes regret to God and denies that he repents. | Explanation: God regrets making Saul king in verses 11 and 35, while Samuel says in verse 29 that Israel's Glory does not repent like a human.
- Exodus 20:5 + Ezekiel 18:20 - Descendants face punishment for ancestral wrongdoing, while Ezekiel rejects inherited guilt. | Explanation: Exodus describes punishment for fathers' iniquity reaching the third and fourth generations of those who hate God. Ezekiel says children do not bear their parents' guilt.
- John 1:18 + Genesis 32:30 - John says no one has seen God; Jacob says he saw God face to face. | Explanation: John states that no one has ever seen God. After wrestling with a mysterious figure, Jacob names the place for having seen God face to face and survived.
- Matthew 5:17-19 + Ephesians 2:14-15 - Jesus affirms the law's continuing force; Ephesians describes Christ abolishing commandments in ordinances. | Explanation: Matthew says not the smallest part of the law passes away until all is accomplished. Ephesians describes Christ abolishing the law of commandments in ordinances.
- Matthew 5:44 + Psalm 139:21-22 - Jesus commands love of enemies; the psalmist expresses hatred of God's enemies. | Explanation: Matthew commands love and prayer for persecutors. The psalmist declares complete hatred of those who hate God.
- John 5:31 + John 8:14 - Jesus makes different statements about the validity of his own testimony. | Explanation: John 5 says testimony about himself alone is not true; John 8 says that even his testimony about himself is true.
- Matthew 10:34-36 + Luke 2:14 - Jesus describes bringing division, while the birth announcement proclaims peace. | Explanation: Matthew records Jesus saying he brings a sword and family divisions rather than peace. Luke's angels proclaim peace on Earth at his birth.
- Matthew 7:7-8 + James 4:3 - One passage promises that asking brings receipt; another explains why requests go unanswered. | Explanation: Matthew states that those who ask receive. James says requests can go unanswered because they are made with wrong motives.
