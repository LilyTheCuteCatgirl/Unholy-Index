# Extraordinary claims

- An-Naml (27:18-19) - Reports an ant warning other ants about Solomon's army and Solomon understanding its words. | Explanation: An ant tells the others to enter their dwellings to avoid being crushed by Solomon's army. Solomon understands the warning and smiles.
- Al-Qamar (54:1-2) - Describes the moon splitting and people dismissing a sign as magic. | Explanation: The passage announces the approach of the Hour and the splitting of the moon, then describes people turning away from a sign and calling it magic. | https://quran.com/en/al-qamar/1/tafsirs
- Al-Baqarah (2:260) - Reports Abraham receiving a demonstration of resurrection involving four birds. | Explanation: Abraham is instructed to take four birds, place portions on hills and call them; the text says they will come to him swiftly.
- Al-Baqarah (2:259) - Reports a man being dead for a hundred years before being restored to life. | Explanation: God causes the man to die for a century, then raises him. His food and drink remain unchanged, and God shows him bones being clothed with flesh.
- Ali 'Imran (3:49) - Reports Jesus fashioning a bird from clay and bringing it to life by God's permission. | Explanation: Jesus makes the shape of a bird from clay and breathes into it; it becomes a bird by God's permission.
- Maryam (19:29-33) - Reports Jesus speaking intelligibly while still in the cradle. | Explanation: The infant answers those questioning Mary and identifies himself as God's servant and a prophet, with obligations of prayer and charity.
- Al-Anbiya (21:68-70) - Reports a fire becoming cool and safe for Abraham at God's command. | Explanation: Abraham's opponents call for him to be burned. God commands the fire to become cool and safe for him.
- An-Naml (27:38-40) - Reports the Queen of Sheba's throne being brought to Solomon before his glance returns. | Explanation: A person possessing knowledge of the Book offers to bring the throne before Solomon's glance returns. Solomon then sees the throne placed before him.
- Ash-Shu'ara (26:63-66) - Reports Moses striking the sea with his staff and the sea splitting into mountain-like masses. | Explanation: Moses strikes the sea at God's instruction. Its divided portions are compared to great mountains, allowing Moses's companions to escape.
- Al-Kahf (18:25-26) - Associates the sleepers in the cave with a stay of 300 years plus nine. | Explanation: The passage gives a duration of 300 years plus nine and says God knows best how long they stayed. This is much longer than a verified human lifespan.
- Al-Qasas (28:31) - Reports Moses's staff moving like a snake, causing him to flee. | Explanation: Moses throws his staff down and sees it moving like a snake. He turns and flees before being reassured.
- Al-Baqarah (2:60) - Reports twelve springs flowing from a rock struck by Moses. | Explanation: Moses is instructed to strike the rock with his staff. Twelve springs gush from it, providing water for the people.
- Al-Baqarah (2:72-73) - Reports a slain person being struck with part of a sacrificed cow as a sign of resurrection. | Explanation: After a killing and a dispute about it, God commands that the victim be struck with part of the cow. The passage presents this as a demonstration of how God brings the dead to life.
- Al-Baqarah (2:65-66) - Reports Sabbath transgressors being commanded to become despised apes. | Explanation: God addresses the Sabbath violators with a command to become apes and describes the punishment as a warning.
- Al-A'raf (7:107-108) - Reports Moses's staff becoming a serpent and his hand becoming white before spectators. | Explanation: Moses throws down his staff, which becomes a visible serpent. He then brings out his hand, which appears white to those watching.
- Al-A'raf (7:143) - Reports God manifesting himself to a mountain, causing it to crumble and Moses to faint. | Explanation: Moses asks to see God. When God manifests himself to the mountain, the mountain crumbles and Moses falls unconscious.
- Al-A'raf (7:171) - Reports a mountain raised above a community like a canopy. | Explanation: The mountain is described as being raised above the people so that they fear it will fall upon them, accompanying a command to uphold revelation.
- Maryam (19:16-22) - Reports Mary conceiving a child despite saying no man has touched her. | Explanation: A messenger announces a son to Mary. She asks how she can have a child without a man having touched her; she subsequently conceives.
- Maryam (19:2-9) - Reports Zachariah being promised a son despite his old age and his wife's infertility. | Explanation: Zachariah describes his weakened bones, old age and barren wife. God promises him a son named Yahya and describes the feat as easy.
- Al-Anbiya (21:81-82) - Reports wind serving Solomon and devils diving and performing work for him. | Explanation: God subjects a powerful wind to Solomon's command and describes devils diving and doing other tasks under divine supervision.
- An-Naml (27:20-28) - Reports a hoopoe delivering detailed information about the kingdom of Sheba. | Explanation: The hoopoe reports a queen, her throne and her people's worship of the Sun. Solomon sends the bird back with a letter.
- Saba (34:12-13) - Reports wind transporting Solomon and jinn constructing objects under his authority. | Explanation: The wind covers a month's journey in the morning and another in the evening. Jinn work for Solomon, making structures, statues and vessels.
- Saba (34:14) - Reports Solomon's death remaining unnoticed by jinn until a creature consumes his staff. | Explanation: The narrative says Solomon's death becomes apparent when a creature of the earth eats his staff and he falls down. The jinn then recognize their ignorance.
- As-Saffat (37:139-145) - Reports Jonah being swallowed by a fish and later cast ashore. | Explanation: A fish swallows Jonah after he loses a drawing of lots. The passage says he would have remained inside until resurrection had he not glorified God, and describes him being cast ashore sick.
- Al-Qamar (54:11-15) - Reports heavenly gates opening with pouring water and springs bursting from Earth during Noah's flood. | Explanation: The passage describes water coming from heaven and Earth, meeting according to a divine decree, and Noah being carried on a vessel of planks and fastenings.
- Al-Isra (17:1) - Reports God's servant being transported between two sanctuaries in one night. | Explanation: The passage glorifies God for taking his servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque in order to show him signs.
- Al-Fil (105:1-5) - Reports flocks of birds pelting the army of the elephant with stones of baked clay. | Explanation: God sends birds in flocks carrying stones against the army of the elephant. The resulting condition of the army is compared to eaten straw.
- Al-Jinn (72:1-2) - Reports a group of jinn listening to the Quran and declaring belief in it. | Explanation: A group of jinn hears the recitation, describes it as wondrous and guiding toward righteousness, and announces belief.
- Al-Ahqaf (46:29-32) - Reports jinn listening to revelation and returning to warn their own people. | Explanation: God directs a group of jinn to listen to the Quran. They return to their people as warners, urging them to respond to God's caller.
- Al-Isra (17:50-52) - Describes resurrection even if people become stones or iron. | Explanation: Those questioning resurrection are told to become stone, iron or something still harder; God will bring them back and call them forth.

# Scientific tensions

- Al-Kahf (18:86) - Describes a traveler reaching the setting place of the sun and finding it setting in a spring. | Explanation: The passage describes Dhul-Qarnayn finding the sun setting in a muddy or hot spring, depending on the reading. The sun is a star approximately 150 million kilometers from Earth and does not physically enter a terrestrial spring. | https://quran.com/18/86 | https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/
- At-Tariq (86:5-7) - Describes human origin from gushing fluid emerging between the backbone and ribs in common translations. | Explanation: The verses describe a gushing fluid emerging between the backbone and ribs. Sperm are produced in the testes, and most seminal fluid originates in pelvic reproductive glands. The Arabic anatomical terms have differing translations. | https://quran.com/86%3A7/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir | https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/semen
- Al-Mulk (67:5) - Describes heavenly lamps and their use as missiles against devils. | Explanation: The verse describes lamps adorning the nearest heaven and being made into missiles against devils. Stars and meteors are distinct astronomical phenomena: meteors arise from material entering Earth's atmosphere. | https://quran.com/fr/al-mulk/5/tafsirs
- An-Nahl (16:15) - Describes mountains being placed on Earth to prevent it from shaking with people. | Explanation: The verse links firm mountains with preventing the Earth from swaying. Mountain-building often occurs in tectonically active regions that experience earthquakes. | https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html
- Al-Ankabut (29:14) - Describes Noah remaining among his people for 950 years before the flood. | Explanation: The passage assigns Noah a period of one thousand years minus fifty among his people. This duration alone greatly exceeds verified human longevity.
- Al-Mu'minun (23:12-14) - Describes embryonic formation as a succession including bones followed by flesh covering them. | Explanation: The sequence describes a drop, a clinging substance, a lump, bones and then flesh covering bones. In human embryology, muscle and skeletal tissues develop in overlapping stages. | https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=23&verse=14
