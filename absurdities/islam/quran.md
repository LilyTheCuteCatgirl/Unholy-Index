# Extraordinary claims

- An-Naml (27:18-19) - Reports an ant warning other ants about Solomon’s army and Solomon understanding its words. | Explanation: Ants communicate with one another, but the extraordinary element is Solomon understanding an ant’s specific warning about his army. The account is framed as a special ability, not an ordinary human capacity.
- Al-Qamar (54:1-2) - Describes the moon splitting; commonly interpreted as a miracle, though some readings associate it with the future. | Explanation: The text describes the moon splitting. A literal physical division of the moon would be an extraordinary astronomical event; many interpretations treat it as a miracle, while others read it as a future sign. | https://quran.com/en/al-qamar/1/tafsirs
# Scientific tensions

- Al-Kahf (18:86) - Describes the sun setting in a spring from a traveler’s viewpoint; a literal astronomical reading conflicts with science. | Explanation: The traveler encounters the sun as though it sets in a spring. A literal physical reading conflicts with astronomy, whereas a description of its apparent position from his viewpoint does not require the sun to enter the spring. | https://quran.com/18/86

# Extraordinary claims

- Al-Baqarah (2:260) - Reports Abraham receiving a demonstration of resurrection involving four birds. | Explanation: The birds are taken and portions placed on hills, after which they come at Abraham's call in the common interpretation. This is presented as a divine demonstration of resurrection; interpretations of the instruction differ.
- Al-Baqarah (2:259) - Reports a man being dead for a hundred years before being restored to life. | Explanation: The account contrasts the passage of a century with the man's perception of a short interval and describes restoration of bones. It presents divine resurrection, not an ordinary human survival mechanism.
- Ali 'Imran (3:49) - Reports Jesus fashioning a bird from clay and bringing it to life by God's permission. | Explanation: The text presents inanimate clay becoming a living bird. This is a miraculous sign explicitly attributed to divine permission rather than a biological process.
- Maryam (19:29-33) - Reports Jesus speaking intelligibly while still in the cradle. | Explanation: The infant delivers a developed statement of his prophetic role. Such speech exceeds normal infant linguistic development; the narrative presents an extraordinary sign.
- Al-Anbiya (21:68-70) - Reports a fire becoming cool and safe for Abraham at God's command. | Explanation: The narrative depicts protection from fire through direct divine command. It is an extraordinary intervention rather than an account of ordinary heat exposure.
- An-Naml (27:38-40) - Reports the Queen of Sheba's throne being brought to Solomon before his glance returns. | Explanation: The account describes extraordinarily rapid transportation of a throne by someone possessing knowledge of the Book. The text gives no ordinary physical mechanism for this feat.
- Ash-Shu'ara (26:63-66) - Reports Moses striking the sea with his staff and the sea splitting into towering parts. | Explanation: The narrative presents a sea crossing made possible by divine intervention. The described division into mountain-like masses of water is an extraordinary event.
- Al-Kahf (18:25-26) - Associates the sleepers in the cave with a stay of 300 years plus nine. | Explanation: The narrative's duration far exceeds ordinary human survival without normal nourishment and activity. The passage also assigns ultimate knowledge of the duration to God; interpretations of the reported number differ.
- Al-Qasas (28:31) - Reports Moses' staff moving like a snake, causing him to flee. | Explanation: The staff behaves like a living serpent after being thrown down. The narrative presents this as a divine sign; it is not an ordinary property of wood.

# Scientific tensions

- At-Tariq (86:5-7) - Describes human origin from gushing fluid emerging from between the backbone and ribs in common translations. | Explanation: If read as the anatomical origin of semen, the description differs from modern reproductive anatomy. Interpretations vary over the referent of the fluid and the Arabic anatomical terms, so the issue is translation-sensitive. | https://quran.com/86%3A7/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir
- Al-Mulk (67:5) - Describes heavenly lamps and their use as missiles against devils. | Explanation: A literal identification of the heavenly lamps with stars used as projectiles conflicts with astronomy. Traditional interpretations can distinguish stars from meteors or describe an unseen spiritual event rather than an astronomical mechanism. | https://quran.com/fr/al-mulk/5/tafsirs
