# Extraordinary claims

- Joshua 10:12-14 - Reports the sun and moon standing still during a battle. | Explanation: Joshua commands the sun and moon to stop. The narrative says the sun delays its setting for about a whole day.
- John 11:43-44 - Reports Jesus raising Lazarus from death and calling him out of his tomb. | Explanation: Lazarus emerges from his tomb still wrapped in burial cloths after Jesus calls him.
- Numbers 22:28-30 - Reports God enabling Balaam's donkey to speak with him. | Explanation: God opens the donkey's mouth; it questions Balaam about his treatment of it, and Balaam replies.
- Genesis 19:26 - Reports Lot's wife becoming a pillar of salt after looking back. | Explanation: The narrative describes a human being transforming into a pillar of salt after looking behind her.
- Exodus 14:21-22 - Reports the sea dividing so Israelites cross on dry ground between walls of water. | Explanation: Moses stretches out his hand; a wind drives the sea back, leaving dry ground with water on both sides.
- Exodus 7:10-12 - Reports staffs transforming into serpents, with Aaron's staff swallowing the others. | Explanation: Aaron and the Egyptian magicians throw down their staffs, which become serpents. Aaron's staff swallows the others.
- 2 Kings 2:11 - Reports Elijah ascending into heaven in a whirlwind as fiery horses and a chariot appear. | Explanation: A chariot and horses of fire separate Elijah from Elisha, and Elijah goes up into heaven in a whirlwind.
- 2 Kings 6:5-7 - Reports Elisha making a lost iron axe head float after throwing wood into water. | Explanation: Elisha throws a piece of wood where the iron fell and makes the iron float. Solid iron is denser than water and ordinarily sinks.
- 2 Kings 13:20-21 - Reports a dead man reviving after touching Elisha's bones. | Explanation: A body thrown into Elisha's tomb touches his bones, comes back to life and stands upright.
- Matthew 12:40 - Jesus refers to Jonah spending three days and nights inside a great fish. | Explanation: Jesus compares Jonah's stay inside a great fish to his own burial. A human cannot breathe normally inside a fish's digestive tract.
- Matthew 14:25-33 - Reports Jesus and briefly Peter walking on the surface of water. | Explanation: Jesus walks across the water toward the boat. Peter also walks on it before becoming afraid and beginning to sink.
- John 2:7-11 - Reports Jesus transforming water into wine at a wedding. | Explanation: Servants fill jars with water. When their contents are served, the steward tastes wine rather than water.
- Matthew 27:51-53 - Reports tombs opening and deceased saints appearing to many after Jesus' resurrection. | Explanation: The narrative describes graves opening, bodies of deceased saints being raised, and the risen saints entering Jerusalem.
- Genesis 3:1-5 - Reports a serpent speaking to the woman about God's command. | Explanation: The serpent questions God's prohibition and disputes the stated consequence of eating the fruit. The woman answers it.
- Genesis 21:1-5 - Reports Sarah bearing Abraham a son in their old age. | Explanation: The narrative says Sarah bears Isaac at the appointed time and Abraham is 100 years old. Genesis 17:17 gives Sarah's age as 90 when the birth is promised.
- Exodus 3:2-4 - Reports a bush burning without being consumed and God speaking from it. | Explanation: Moses sees a bush on fire that is not consumed. God calls to him from the bush.
- Exodus 4:6-7 - Reports Moses's hand becoming diseased and then being instantly restored. | Explanation: Moses puts his hand inside his garment twice. It first emerges white with disease and then returns to its normal condition.
- Exodus 16:13-15 - Reports an unfamiliar food appearing on the ground to feed the Israelites. | Explanation: After the morning dew disappears, a fine substance covers the ground. Moses identifies it as food supplied by God.
- Exodus 17:5-6 - Reports water flowing from a rock struck by Moses. | Explanation: God tells Moses to strike a rock at Horeb with his staff; water comes out for the people to drink.
- Numbers 17:8 - Reports Aaron's detached staff growing buds, blossoms and ripe almonds overnight. | Explanation: The staff placed before the testimony produces buds, flowers and almonds by the next day, bypassing ordinary plant growth times.
- Joshua 6:15-20 - Reports Jericho's wall collapsing when trumpets are sounded and the people shout. | Explanation: After repeated circuits of the city, priests sound their trumpets and the people shout; the wall falls and the Israelites enter.
- Judges 6:36-40 - Reports dew appearing selectively on a fleece and then on the ground. | Explanation: Gideon requests two signs. First the fleece is wet while the ground remains dry; the next night the fleece stays dry while the ground becomes wet.
- Judges 14:5-6 - Reports Samson tearing apart a lion with his bare hands after receiving divine strength. | Explanation: The spirit of God comes upon Samson and he tears the attacking lion apart without a weapon.
- 1 Kings 17:4-6 - Reports ravens delivering bread and meat to Elijah morning and evening. | Explanation: God instructs Elijah to hide by a stream and appoints ravens to supply food twice daily.
- 1 Kings 17:14-16 - Reports flour and oil remaining available despite continued use during famine. | Explanation: The widow's flour and oil do not run out, in accordance with Elijah's announcement of God's promise.
- 1 Kings 17:17-24 - Reports Elijah reviving a widow's deceased son. | Explanation: Elijah prays and stretches himself over the child three times. The child's life returns, and Elijah brings him back to his mother.
- 1 Kings 18:33-39 - Reports divine fire consuming a sacrifice, wood, stones, dust and water. | Explanation: Elijah's altar is drenched with water before he prays. Fire consumes the offering and surrounding materials, including the water.
- 2 Kings 4:1-7 - Reports oil multiplying until numerous borrowed vessels are filled. | Explanation: A widow pours oil from her available supply into borrowed vessels. The flow stops only when there are no more vessels to fill.
- 2 Kings 4:32-37 - Reports Elisha restoring a deceased child to life. | Explanation: Elisha prays and lies over the child's body. The child warms, sneezes seven times and opens his eyes.
- 2 Kings 20:8-11 - Reports a shadow moving backward ten steps as a sign of Hezekiah's recovery. | Explanation: At Isaiah's prayer, the shadow moves backward ten steps on the steps of Ahaz, contrary to its preceding direction.
- Daniel 3:24-27 - Reports three men surviving a fiery furnace without injury. | Explanation: The men are seen walking in the furnace with a fourth figure. They emerge unharmed, without even the smell of fire on them.
- Daniel 5:5-6 - Reports humanlike fingers appearing and writing on a palace wall. | Explanation: During the feast, fingers of a human hand appear and write on the wall. The king sees them and becomes terrified.
- Daniel 6:22-23 - Reports Daniel surviving a lions' den because an angel shuts the lions' mouths. | Explanation: Daniel attributes his survival to an angel sent by God. He is lifted from the den without injury.
- Matthew 1:18-25 - Reports Mary's pregnancy occurring before sexual relations with Joseph through the Holy Spirit. | Explanation: Mary is found pregnant before the couple come together. An angel attributes the conception to the Holy Spirit, and Joseph does not have relations with her before the birth.
- Matthew 8:23-27 - Reports Jesus stopping a storm by rebuking wind and sea. | Explanation: Jesus rebukes the winds and sea, and a great calm follows. The disciples express astonishment that both obey him.
- Matthew 14:15-21 - Reports five loaves and two fish feeding thousands with leftovers. | Explanation: Jesus distributes five loaves and two fish through the disciples. About 5,000 men, besides women and children, eat, and twelve baskets of fragments remain.
- Matthew 17:24-27 - Reports Jesus predicting a coin in a fish's mouth to pay the temple tax. | Explanation: Jesus directs Peter to catch a fish and says he will find a coin in its mouth sufficient for their tax. The passage records the instruction, not Peter's subsequent catch.
- Matthew 21:18-22 - Reports Jesus causing a fig tree to wither after finding no fruit. | Explanation: Jesus declares that no fruit will grow on the tree again. Matthew reports that the tree withers immediately.
- Mark 5:35-43 - Reports Jesus raising Jairus's daughter after news of her death. | Explanation: The household receives news that the girl is dead. Jesus takes her hand, commands her to rise, and she gets up and walks.
- Luke 7:12-15 - Reports Jesus reviving a widow's son during a funeral procession. | Explanation: Jesus stops the procession and addresses the dead young man. He sits up and begins speaking.
- John 9:6-7 - Reports a man born blind gaining sight after washing off clay made with saliva. | Explanation: Jesus applies clay mixed with his saliva to the man's eyes and sends him to wash. The man returns able to see.
- Acts 2:1-4 - Reports tongues like fire and sudden speech in other languages. | Explanation: The disciples see tongues resembling fire resting on them and begin speaking in other languages as the Spirit enables them.
- Acts 8:39-40 - Reports the Spirit carrying Philip away after a baptism. | Explanation: After the baptism, the Spirit takes Philip away, and the Ethiopian no longer sees him. Philip is subsequently found at Azotus.
- Acts 9:36-41 - Reports Peter reviving Tabitha after her body has been prepared for burial. | Explanation: Peter prays and tells Tabitha to rise. She opens her eyes and sits up, and Peter presents her alive.
- Acts 12:6-10 - Reports an angel freeing Peter from chains and a prison with a gate opening by itself. | Explanation: An angel wakes Peter, his chains fall off, and the iron gate leading to the city opens of its own accord.

# Scientific tensions

- Genesis 1:11-19 - Places vegetation before the creation of the heavenly lights. | Explanation: The narrative places vegetation on day three and the heavenly lights on day four. Astronomical and geological evidence places the Sun's formation long before terrestrial plants. | https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/
- Genesis 5:25-27 - Assigns Methuselah a lifespan of 969 years. | Explanation: Methuselah's stated lifespan is 969 years, far beyond any verified human lifespan.
- Genesis 7:19-24 - Describes floodwaters covering all high mountains and destroying land animals outside the ark. | Explanation: The account says all high mountains under heaven are covered and land-dwelling creatures outside the ark die. Geological and biological evidence does not support such a global flood in human history.
- Leviticus 11:6 - Classifies the hare as chewing the cud while lacking divided hooves. | Explanation: Hares reingest nutrient-rich fecal pellets through cecotrophy. They do not regurgitate cud as ruminants do.
- Genesis 5:3-5 - Assigns Adam a lifespan of 930 years. | Explanation: The genealogy says Adam lives 930 years. Verified human longevity does not approach that age.
- Genesis 9:28-29 - Assigns Noah a lifespan of 950 years. | Explanation: Noah is said to live 350 years after the flood and 950 years in total, exceeding any verified human lifespan.
- Leviticus 11:5 - Classifies the rock hyrax as chewing the cud. | Explanation: The text classifies the animal as cud-chewing. Rock hyraxes are not ruminants and do not regurgitate and rechew cud.
- Genesis 30:37-39 - Reports Jacob breeding marked offspring using striped rods placed before mating animals. | Explanation: Jacob places peeled rods before the animals while they mate; the narrative associates this with marked offspring. Coat patterns are determined by genetics, not by seeing stripes while mating.
