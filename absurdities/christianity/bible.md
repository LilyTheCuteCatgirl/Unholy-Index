# Extraordinary claims

- Joshua 10:12-14 - Reports the sun and moon standing still during a battle, presented as divine intervention. | Explanation: The narrative describes the sun and moon stopping during a battle. A literal reading involves an extraordinary astronomical event outside ordinary celestial motion; the passage presents it as a miracle, not a scientific account.
- John 11:43-44 - Reports Jesus raising Lazarus from death and calling him out of his tomb. | Explanation: The narrative presents Jesus bringing a dead man back to life. The extraordinary claim is the reversal of death, understood in the text as a miracle rather than an ordinary biological event.
- Numbers 22:28-30 - Reports God enabling Balaam’s donkey to speak and converse with him. | Explanation: The donkey speaks in human language and converses with Balaam, beyond the normal linguistic abilities of donkeys. The narrative explicitly attributes this ability to divine intervention.
# Scientific tensions

- Genesis 1:11-19 - Places vegetation before the creation of the sun; a literal chronological reading conflicts with natural history. | Explanation: Vegetation appears before the heavenly lights in the narrative sequence. A literal chronological interpretation conflicts with the scientific understanding that the sun predates plants on Earth; nonliteral readings need not make that chronological claim.

# Extraordinary claims

- Genesis 19:26 - Reports Lot's wife becoming a pillar of salt after looking back. | Explanation: The narrative depicts a human body suddenly turning into salt. This is not an ordinary biological transformation; the story presents it within divine judgment.
- Exodus 14:21-22 - Reports the sea dividing so Israelites cross on dry ground between walls of water. | Explanation: The narrative presents a passage through the sea created by a divinely directed wind. Naturalistic proposals exist, but the described crossing between walls of water is an extraordinary event.
- Exodus 7:10-12 - Reports staffs transforming into serpents, with Aaron's staff swallowing the others. | Explanation: The narrative depicts wooden staffs becoming living animals. The transformation is presented as a supernatural contest, not a process known to biology.
- 2 Kings 2:11 - Reports Elijah ascending into heaven in a whirlwind as fiery horses and a chariot appear. | Explanation: The account describes Elijah being taken away in a whirlwind without an ordinary death. Its extraordinary character is part of the prophetic narrative.
- 2 Kings 6:5-7 - Reports Elisha making a lost iron axe head float after throwing wood into water. | Explanation: Iron normally sinks in water unless supported or displaced by a buoyant structure. The narrative portrays the recovered axe head as an extraordinary act of the prophet.
- 2 Kings 13:20-21 - Reports a dead man reviving after touching Elisha's bones. | Explanation: The story attributes renewed life to contact with a deceased prophet's remains. Such contact is not a known mechanism for reversing death; the narrative depicts a miracle.
- Matthew 12:40 - Jesus refers to Jonah spending three days and nights inside a great fish. | Explanation: Ordinary human respiration and survival would not be possible for days inside a fish's digestive system. Jesus uses the story as a sign of his own burial; the reference may be read figuratively.
- Matthew 14:25-33 - Reports Jesus and briefly Peter walking on the surface of water. | Explanation: Unsupported human bodies cannot ordinarily walk on liquid water. The narrative presents the event as supernatural rather than as an ordinary physical ability.
- John 2:7-11 - Reports Jesus transforming water into wine at a wedding. | Explanation: Wine normally requires organic ingredients and fermentation. The narrative reports an immediate transformation and explicitly identifies it as a sign.
- Matthew 27:51-53 - Reports tombs opening and deceased saints appearing to many after Jesus' resurrection. | Explanation: The account depicts multiple deceased people returning to life and entering the holy city. It is an extraordinary resurrection narrative rather than an established biological process.

# Scientific tensions

- Genesis 5:25-27 - Assigns Methuselah a lifespan of 969 years. | Explanation: This lifespan far exceeds verified human longevity. The tension arises if the age is read as ordinary chronological years; symbolic or schematic interpretations do not require a biological claim.
- Genesis 7:19-24 - Describes floodwaters covering all high mountains and destroying land-dwelling animals outside the ark. | Explanation: A literal recent global flood covering every mountain conflicts with geological and biological evidence. Local-flood or literary interpretations do not require such a worldwide event.
- Leviticus 11:6 - Classifies the hare as chewing the cud while lacking divided hooves. | Explanation: Hares practice cecotrophy, reingesting special fecal pellets, rather than true ruminant cud-chewing. The ancient dietary classification may describe observable behavior rather than modern zoological categories.
