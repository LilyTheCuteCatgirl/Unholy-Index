# Slavery

- Al-Baqarah (2:178) - Distinguishes free persons, slaves and women in its formulation of retaliation for murder; legal applications differ.
- An-Nisa (4:25) - Regulates marriage to enslaved believing women and assigns them half the punishment of free women for sexual misconduct.
- An-Nahl (16:71) - Uses the unequal distribution of wealth between people and those they own as part of a theological argument.
- An-Nahl (16:75) - Uses the powerlessness of an owned slave as a contrast with a free person in a parable.
- An-Nur (24:32) - Encourages marriage of unmarried people, including male and female slaves, while recognizing slave ownership.
- An-Nur (24:33) - Regulates contracts for slaves to purchase freedom and prohibits forcing enslaved women into prostitution, without abolishing slavery.
- Ar-Rum (30:28) - Uses the assumed inequality between owners and slaves as an analogy against associating partners with God.

# Sexual slavery and forced marriage

- An-Nisa (4:3) - Allows up to four wives and refers to enslaved women as an alternative.
- An-Nisa (4:24) - Exempts enslaved female captives from the general prohibition concerning married women, traditionally permitting sexual access despite prior marriages.
- Al-Muminun (23:5-7) - Permits sexual relations with spouses and those whom one's right hand possesses, traditionally understood to include enslaved women.
- Al-Ahzab (33:50) - Gives Muhammad permission concerning wives and enslaved women acquired through war, alongside special marital privileges.
- Al-Ahzab (33:52) - Restricts Muhammad from taking additional wives while making an exception for enslaved women.
- Al-Maarij (70:29-31) - Exempts spouses and those whom one's right hand possesses from its prohibition of sexual relations.
